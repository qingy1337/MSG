# Future PvE: Strikes, Loot, and Armor

> Design direction only. None of the systems in this document should be treated
> as implemented or scheduled yet.

## PvE modes

The game should retain **Endless PvE** and add **Strikes** as a separate mode.

### Endless PvE

- Keep the existing procedural, floor-based survival mode.
- Preserve its escalating enemies, endless progression, and replayable generated
  architecture.
- It should remain the mode for seeing how far a team can survive.

### Strikes

- Strikes are fixed, deliberately authored cooperative missions.
- Their terrain, route, environment, storyline, interactive components, major
  encounters, and final boss do not change between runs.
- They should be enjoyable to replay because the combat, traversal, spectacle,
  encounter mechanics, and pacing are intrinsically fun—not because the level is
  rearranged.
- Familiarity should create mastery: players learn routes, encounters, boss
  mechanics, shortcuts, and efficient team strategies over repeated runs.
- Each strike should have a distinct identity and memorable, polished set pieces.

## Strike rewards

Completing a strike can award:

- Coins
- Strike-specific weapon skins
- Armor drops
- Potentially other strike-specific collectibles later

Some skins should **never be sold in the shop**. Each of these skins belongs to a
particular strike and has a low chance to drop when that strike is completed. This
creates a meaningful reason to master and repeatedly run a favorite strike.

Reward tuning should respect the player's time. Rare rewards should feel exciting,
but the system may eventually need bad-luck protection so an unlucky player is not
locked out indefinitely.

## Armor and loadouts

Armor should become part of the player's loadout. Each armor piece has:

- A recognizable visual design
- A defined range for several gameplay stats
- Base stat tendencies appropriate to that armor
- A randomly rolled distribution within those ranges whenever it drops

Two copies of the same armor can therefore support different builds without losing
the item's identity. Randomness should adjust the armor's intended stat profile,
not turn every drop into an unrelated item.

Possible stat categories include:

- Health or resilience
- Health-regeneration behavior
- Movement or handling
- Grenade recharge/capacity
- Weapon handling or reload behavior
- Ability recharge, once abilities exist

Exact stats, caps, armor slots, and stacking rules remain undecided. PvP balance
must be considered separately before armor bonuses are allowed there.

## Exotic armor

Exotic armor should change what the player can **do**, rather than merely providing
larger stat bonuses. An exotic perk might add a new action, alter an existing
mechanic, or enable a distinctive playstyle.

Examples of the intended design space—not committed items—include:

- A directional air dash
- A grenade converted into a deployable shield
- A ground slam after falling far enough
- A precision kill that briefly reveals or weakens nearby enemies
- A revive that releases a protective or damaging pulse

Exotics should create interesting build decisions and visible gameplay changes.
They should not simply be mandatory, numerically superior versions of ordinary
armor. Loadout rules may need to limit how many exotic pieces can be equipped.

## Guiding principles

- Endless PvE and Strikes serve different purposes; neither replaces the other.
- Strikes are authored and consistent across runs.
- Repetition is supported by mastery, strong combat, memorable encounters, and
  desirable loot.
- Strike-exclusive cosmetics make specific missions worth replaying.
- Random armor rolls create build variety while preserving item identity.
- Exotic armor introduces capabilities and playstyles, not only stat inflation.
- Avoid pay-to-win progression and avoid invalidating the existing weapon sandbox.
